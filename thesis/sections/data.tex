\section{Simulator and Data}
\label{sec:data}

Testing whether a model has learned physics needs the physics to be known exactly, frame
by frame. Real LPTEM video does not come with that: particle sizes and positions in real
movies are themselves estimates, and no real movie states which growth law it follows.
We therefore train and evaluate on simulated video with an exact answer key, and use real
published movies only to test transfer (Section~\ref{sec:res-real}).

\subsection{The LPTEM Simulator}
\label{sec:simulator}

The simulator has two separate parts: a physics engine that produces the ground truth,
and an image-formation model that turns it into pixels. The model only ever sees the
pixels; the evaluation only ever uses the ground truth.

\paragraph{Physics.} Particles diffuse as Brownian walkers with a shared diffusion
coefficient $D$ inside reflecting walls, with an optional stage drift common to all
particles. Five processes set how their radii evolve:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{diffusion-limited growth}, $r \propto t^{1/3}$;
  \item \emph{reaction-limited growth}, $r \propto t^{1/2}$;
  \item \emph{static}, constant radii;
  \item \emph{Ostwald ripening}, a coupled, mass-conserving Lifshitz--Slyozov--Wagner
    population in which small particles feed large ones;
  \item \emph{coalescence}, volume-conserving merging on contact, the only process in
    which particles interact and the only source of discrete, exactly timed events.
\end{itemize}
In the two growth processes every particle has its own rate constant, pinned to its
initial radius so that all particles follow the same power law from a common nucleation
time. Without this the growth equations contract the size distribution to a single
radius within one clip, and comparing size distributions would degenerate to comparing
means. Particle size is set by a target area fraction rather than by the growth law, so
that particles stay separable and every probe failure can be attributed to the model,
not to an answer that is not visible.

\paragraph{Image formation.} Frames are rendered with a re-implementation of the image
formation of the LPTEMsimulator~\cite{lptemsim}: Beer--Lambert attenuation through a
stack of gold, water and silicon nitride with their inelastic mean free paths, a bulging
liquid-thickness field, Poisson shot noise at the given electron dose, Gaussian read
noise, and a detector transfer function calibrated to real video. The particle geometry
is ours: spheres with a correct chord thickness, where the upstream code draws nanorods.
Imaging conditions are randomised over ranges, not fitted to one experiment: the
criterion was that real experimental video lies inside the generated range on every
image statistic that survives the unknown contrast stretch of published frames
(apparent radius, relative temporal change, and the ratio of temporal to spatial noise).

\paragraph{Validation of the answer key.} A regression suite grades the ground truth
itself on freshly generated clips: growth exponents to $\pm 0.001$, the pooled MSD
against $4Dt$ over the lag range where it is valid, coverage caps, no particle labelled
below the visibility floor, no leak from brightness to size, larger particles diffusing
more slowly, ripening that coarsens, and timed, volume-conserving merges. Three of the
simulator's statistics were initially tuned to artefacts of their own estimators; each
was caught by pointing the estimator at clips whose answer was known.

\subsection{The Synthetic Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

The dataset contains 2000 clips of 100 frames at $512 \times 512$\,px, 400 per process,
in two strata:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{physics} stratum (320 clips per process): 6--16 particles of
    8--22\,px radius per clip, at doses of 6--75\,e/px. All pass/fail criteria are
    decided on this stratum;
  \item the \emph{experiment} stratum (80 clips per process): the imaging conditions of
    real video, at most 2\,\% coverage and doses of 8--12\,e/px, so that real data falls
    inside the training distribution.
\end{itemize}
Each clip stores its frames, every particle's position and radius at every frame, the
merge events (1035 in total, about 2.6 per coalescence clip) and every simulation
parameter. The mean liquid thickness is fixed at 150\,nm. An audit of the full run found
no unreadable or empty clip; on average 0.27\,\% of pixels are black (worst clip
2.89\,\%), which is physical: at the lowest dose behind the thickest liquid a pixel
receives about four electrons, so a few percent receive none.

For the model, each clip is centre-cropped to $256 \times 256$\,px (cropped, never
resized, so that one pixel stays one detector pixel) and cut into two 64-frame windows
(frames 0--63 and 36--99). The clips are split 1600 / 200 / 200 into training,
validation and test sets, by clip, with one fixed seed for every experiment in this
thesis.

\subsection{Real Published Movies}
\label{sec:real-data}

Raw LPTEM recordings are rarely published. We therefore collected the supplementary
movies of published LPTEM papers on particle growth, diffusion, assembly and
coalescence: 132 movies from 34 papers, under their open licences. A movie made for
display often breaks two premises of the pipeline, that one pixel is one detector pixel
and one frame is one time step, so each movie was screened before use. A movie passes if
it shows white shot noise on its own pixel grid, has grey levels, at least 16 clean
frames and at least 256\,px, and contains a $256 \times 256$ region free of overlays.
Ten of the 132 pass; most were rescaled for display. We use 8 movies from 7 papers~\cite{woehl2014,son2022,robertson2018,welch2016,dachraoui2023,wang2019,kim2016},
together with the Au nanorod video released with SAM-EM~\cite{samem}, which is also the
video the simulator's imaging conditions were checked against.
