\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Liquid-phase transmission electron microscopy (LPTEM) films nanoparticles as they move,
grow and merge in liquid, but its frames are dominated by shot noise, and machine
learning for it has so far been descriptive. This thesis asks whether a self-supervised
video model, trained on internet video and never shown a microscope, learns the physics
of nanoparticles well enough to forecast it. We build a simulator of LPTEM video with an
exact answer key, encode its frames with a frozen V-JEPA~2 encoder, forecast the latent
states, and read particle size, growth exponent, diffusion and merges off the forecasts
with trained probes, against baselines and ceilings and under criteria fixed before each
result. The frozen encoder reads size, area and diffusion from single frames at
$R^2 \approx 0.9$ and at the mean squared displacement of a perfect detector; adapting it
with LoRA reads them markedly better, and with a tenth of the labels matches the frozen
encoder on all of them. Deterministic forecasts of the encoder's tokens cannot decode to
correct physics: half the particles leave their token in a single step, so the optimal
single guess erases them. A position-free state, the frame average of the tokens, can be
forecast without labels; a linear forecaster on it carries each clip's growth beyond every
trend-blind baseline, and its remaining exponent error is what eight to sixteen noisy
frames allow. A pixel-space forecaster trained on the same clips erases the particles
instead, and the growth read off its frames is as large on static clips as on growing ones. Forecast quality degrades strongly with horizon and mildly with electron
dose. Prediction error detects particle merges as they happen but does not anticipate
them, and simple baselines detect them as well. None of these results transfers to
published real LPTEM movies, which lie outside the simulated feature distribution. The
model learns what is predictable, growth, and not what is not, where each particle goes.

\medskip\noindent{\raggedright\textbf{Keywords:} Liquid-Phase Transmission Electron Microscopy,
Self-Supervised Learning, Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures, Latent World Models,
Video Forecasting, Nanoparticle Dynamics, Simulation-Based Evaluation.\par}
